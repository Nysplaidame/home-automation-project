Meals
  • Protein at every meal, palm-to-two-palms sized — protect this floor first as portions shrink; let carbs and fat take almost all of the cut, not protein.
  • Plate method for lunch/dinner: ½ non-starchy veg, ¼ protein, ¼ complex carb, unsaturated fat (olive oil, nuts, avocado, fatty fish).
  • Protein-forward breakfast (Greek yogurt, small unsweetened muesli, ground flax, almonds worked well for you) — never carbs alone. Lean toward whole eggs over whites when eggs are involved. For yogurt specifically: real Greek yogurt or skyr (check the label says "strained," not "Greek style") gives roughly double the protein of natural yogurt at similar calories — best protein-per-calorie choice for this meal.
  • A glass of water ~20–30 min before your larger meals.
  • Eat veg/protein before carbs within a meal where practical; never eat carbs alone.
  • A 10–15 min walk after your largest carb meal of the day.
Night routine
  • Morning light exposure within 30–60 min of waking (tracked as a habit) — the actual lever for shifting your rhythm earlier.
  • Dim light and reading (not screens) starting ~2 hours before bed.
  • Cyclic sighing (two quick inhales, one long slow exhale, a few minutes) as a wind-down tool.
  • Bed is for sleep only — if not asleep within ~20 min, get up, calm dim-light activity, return when sleepy.
  • If training that day, keep a 3–4+ hour buffer before bed — the real danger zone is the last ~60–90 minutes before bed, not the evening broadly.
  • Target: asleep by 23:30, up at 7:00.
Worth knowing
  • Thirst is often mistaken for hunger — if hunger feels sudden or off-schedule, water first.
  • A small amount of honey or similar is fine when paired with a protein/fat/fiber meal — treat it as a personal experiment.
  • Optional, low priority: nasal breathing at rest during the day, and brief "exercise snacks" (5 min bursts — stairs, bodyweight squats) opportunistically through the day for extra glucose-control benefit.

Recovery & resilience

  • Isometric holds, 2-3x/week — two purposes, two intensities. For blood pressure (strongest evidence of any exercise mode): wall squat, ~30-45s at moderate effort, breathing through it. For tendon resilience specifically: hold at the actual sticking point of a lift (bottom of a weighted dip, mid-pull of a pull-up) at high effort (~70%+), since the adaptation is joint-angle specific — a generic hold at an easy angle mostly won't transfer to your lifts.
  • Cyclic sighing / physiological sigh, a few minutes daily if useful — two quick nasal inhales, one long slow exhale, repeated. Well-evidenced for reducing physiological arousal and improving mood; a genuine wind-down tool given your sleep-onset difficulty, distinct from and complementary to the dim-light/reading routine.
  • Focused-attention practice, optional — a few minutes of sustained, undistracted fixation on a neutral point. Real evidence for improving attentional control under pressure. Lower priority than the above two, but genuinely evidence-backed if you want it.

Extra technique notes

  • Carries over crunches for core work — farmer's or suitcase carries (heavy weight in one or both hands, walk with tall posture and braced core) build real functional core strength without repeated spinal flexion. Worth knowing: repeated spinal flexion under load (situps, crunches) is linked to disc issues over time by spine biomechanics research — planks, dead bugs, and carries train the same core without that repeated-flexion pattern.
  • Grip/forearm work is mostly covered already by your weighted pull-ups and dips, but a couple of minutes of direct grip work (dead hangs for time, or simply gripping the bar hard) at the end of a session is low-cost and reinforces the pull-up standard below.
  • Pull-up form check — pull to your chest, not just your chin, by leaning back slightly and driving elbows down and back (a rowing motion) rather than pulling straight up. Retract your shoulder blades before you pull, keep your body in one tight line rather than swinging. This gets more out of every rep you're already doing.

Sleep protocol (CBT-I)

You've already done the lifestyle layer — morning light, dim evening light, reading over screens. Still not asleep until ~1am despite that means it's worth the next layer, which targets learned/conditioned sleep-onset patterns rather than environment.
  • What it is — cognitive behavioral therapy for insomnia. The actual first-line clinical treatment for insomnia, ahead of medication, per every major sleep-medicine body. Meta-analysis of 20 RCTs found it improves sleep-onset latency by ~19 min and sleep efficiency by ~10 percentage points, with effects holding at follow-up.
  • Stimulus control — bed is for sleep only (not reading, not phone, not lying awake worrying). If not asleep within ~20 minutes, get up, do something calm in dim light, only return when sleepy. Breaks the learned association between "being in bed" and "being awake and frustrated."
  • Sleep restriction — temporarily limit time in bed closer to your actual average sleep duration to build sleep pressure, then gradually extend as efficiency improves. This is the counterintuitive, most effective component — but also the one most worth doing with structure (app or professional) rather than freelancing, since it causes short-term daytime sleepiness and you're a parent who needs to stay alert.
  • Free resource — CBT-I Coach app (built by the VA, no affiliation/commercial angle, structures the above into a guided program).
  • Your existing sleep/bedtime log in this app already functions as the sleep diary CBT-I is built around — the data's already being collected either way.

Weekly

Schedule
  • Mon — Calisthenics: Pair A (weighted pull-up ↔ weighted dip), Pair B (weighted/leverage push-up ↔ row). 3–5 sets, 3–6 reps, 90s–2min rest between exercises. Then 1–2 back-off sets (8–15 reps, lighter) on pull-up/dip for extra definition. Finish with one isometric hold.
  • Tue — Cardio (run or skip — skipping already is a plyometric exercise). Add 2-3 sets of 5-8 jump squats or lateral bounds beforehand, quality over quantity, full recovery between sets.
  • Wed — Calisthenics, repeat Mon's structure.
  • Thu — Cardio. Same optional plyo pairing as Tuesday if recovered well.
  • Fri — Rest or light walk — protects the buffer before the weights day.
  • Sat — Weights: squat, Romanian deadlift, overhead press. Same 3–6 rep double-progression approach; start conservative on load the first couple weeks. Add isolation work here (bicep curl, lateral raise, tricep pushdown, calf raise) for definition — this is the easiest venue for it given equipment access. Optional isometric finisher.
  • Sun — Full rest, non-negotiable.
Notes
  • Pre-workout carbs matter most on cardio days — 20–30g fast carbs (juice, banana, dates) 30–45 min before, since the exercise itself consumes the glucose and blunts any crash. Less critical before calisthenics/weights days.
  • Don't stack a hard leg/lower-body day directly against a hard run day early on, while recovery capacity is still building.
  • Walk on true rest days — useful for fat loss, doesn't compete with recovery.
  • Notice patterns across the week (energy, hunger, sleep, cravings) rather than reacting to any single day.

Monthly

  • Progress photos, waist measurement, and weigh-in — trust the trend across months, not any single reading.
  • Re-check whether portions or training need a small adjustment — as you lose weight your maintenance needs drop too, so the same plan produces a shrinking deficit over time. This is expected, not failure.
  • Check for bedtime/training-buffer drift — early nights are easy to lose gradually without noticing.

Variable — lunch (add to the whey scoop)

  • Carb: pick one — beans/lentils, or a moderate portion of bread/couscous, not all three.
  • Veg: greens, salad. Optional: hummus, olives.
Variable — dinner (fully open)
  • Protein: ~150g chicken or fish.
  • Carb: rice or potato, cooled/reheated when convenient for the resistant-starch benefit.
  • Veg: whatever's on hand.
Worked example lands ~2,010 kcal / ~179g protein — a ~390-640 kcal deficit against your ~2,400-2,650 maintenance estimate. If a day feels short, the variable portions are the lever to adjust, not the fixed items.

Macros & supplements reference

Daily targets (~85kg)
  • Protein: 170–195g (2.0–2.3 g/kg) — the one number that shouldn't shrink with portions.
  • Fiber: 30–35g, prioritizing soluble fiber (oats, legumes, psyllium) for cholesterol and glucose control.
  • Carbs: fill remaining intake with low-glycemic-load, whole-food sources; always paired, never alone.
  • Fat: ~25–30% of intake, favoring unsaturated sources over saturated.
Supplements
  • Creatine monohydrate — 3–5g/day, no loading phase needed.
  • Protein powder — as needed to close the gap to your protein target, not a substitute for whole food.
  • Fish oil — 1–2g combined EPA/DHA, mainly for triglycerides.
  • Resistant starch — food sources (cooled/reheated rice or potatoes, green banana, legumes, oats) are good for the glycemic-pairing benefits already built into your meals. Separately, 40g/day of a resistant starch powder (~65-70g/4-5 tbsp Hi-Maize, or ~5 tbsp raw potato starch — dosed by the ~90-175 kcal it actually adds, since it behaves like fiber not regular carb) is a dose specifically shown across multiple RCTs, MRI-confirmed, to reduce visceral fat. Start at ~1 tbsp/day and build up over 1-2 weeks to avoid GI discomfort — food alone won't reach the full 40g dose, so this is worth its own addition given visceral fat is a priority for you.
  • Magnesium / vitamin D — worth asking your GP about given bloodwork, don't self-dose without that context.
  • Berberine — flagged as a question for your doctor given medication interactions and your family history; don't self-start.

Pitfalls to watch for

  • Weighing daily and reacting to normal 1–2kg noise from water/sodium/glycogen.
  • All-or-nothing thinking after one "bad" meal or missed session.
  • Letting portion cuts shrink protein along with everything else.
  • Not expecting the deficit to shrink naturally as you lose weight.
  • Sleep debt quietly undoing appetite control — probably your single biggest lever given your sleep pattern.
  • Comparing your pace to social-media transformation timelines.
  • Training hard without enough recovery, which quietly reduces unconscious daily movement (NEAT).
  • No pre-decided plan for social/eating-out situations — the most commonly reported real-world derailer.
Stages to expect
  • Week 1–2: fast water-weight drop, not fat — feels great, sets unrealistic pace expectations.
  • Week 3–5: "the stall" — measurements plateau even as fat loss continues; the most common quitting point.
  • Week 6–10: motivation dip as novelty fades — systems need to take over from willpower.
  • Month 3–4: metabolic adaptation — same effort yields less; normal physiology, may need a small adjustment.
  • Ongoing: social/environmental pressure — the top reported reason for derailment long-term.
  • Late-stage: the "good enough" plateau, as visible results reduce urgency right when close to goal.

For your GP

  • Fasting glucose, HbA1c, and fasting insulin — given family T2D history; HbA1c alone can miss early insulin resistance.
  • The reactive-hypoglycemia-like pattern (pre-lunch weakness, afternoon slump after high-carb breakfast) — a useful early signal alongside the bloodwork.
  • The night-owl-plus-early-waking sleep pattern, given it's a bit more specific than typical chronotype variation.
  • Ask whether liver enzymes are worth checking alongside the glucose/insulin panel — fatty liver disease is closely linked to the exact risk cluster already being investigated (visceral fat, insulin resistance, family T2D history), and prevalence rises sharply with age.
